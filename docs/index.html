The idea

Move complexity from learning to transport.

The normalizing flow represents uncertainty in an auxiliary posterior. GOTHAM carries that uncertainty through the nonlinear physics.

What is new

Not “NF + homotopy”. A different division of labor.

Change the learning target

The NF learns the tractable auxiliary posterior rather than the difficult nonlinear posterior.

Preserve sample identity

GOTHAM matches a sample-specific inverse coordinate instead of driving all samples to a common inverse root.

Keep density tracking

The finite-order transport remains probabilistically testable through its Jacobian and posterior agreement metrics.

Potential applications

Where this structure may be useful next.

Motivated applications, not experimentally validated claims of the current paper.

Nonlinear system identification

Acoustic, structural and vibroacoustic models with interpretable physical parameters.

Calibration with uncertainty

Inverse problems with strongly correlated or anisotropic parameter posteriors.

Expensive nonlinear models

Cases where a useful local surrogate is cheap but direct posterior learning is costly.

Experiment design

Select excitation and observation geometry before inference to expose identifiable directions.

Local multibasin inference

Use several local transports when a single globally regular map is unrealistic.

Hybrid digital twins

Combine inexpensive surrogate uncertainty with physics-based nonlinear correction.
